# Bala Kanda (Book I): canon reference

Cited as `Griffith <canto>`; see README for the numbering and the status marks. Everything in
this section is **verified** against Griffith unless marked otherwise.

---

## Part A. The frame, Ayodhya and the birth of the princes (Griffith I to XXI)

### Sequence
| Griffith | What happens |
|---|---|
| I | Valmiki asks the sage Narada who is the ideal man; Narada names Rama and tells the whole story in brief. |
| II | At the Tamasa river a hunter kills one of a pair of curlews; Valmiki's grief becomes his first verse (the *shloka*). Brahma tells him to compose the Ramayana. |
| III | Valmiki sees the whole story in meditation. |
| IV | He teaches it to the twins Kusha and Lava, who recite it to the hermits and before Rama at his horse sacrifice. *(Griffith's note: cantos I-IV are an introduction, probably by a later hand.)* |
| V-VII | Ayodhya, King Dasharatha and his ministers. |
| VIII-X | Childless Dasharatha; Rishyasringa is brought to Anga to end its drought and marries Santa, Dasharatha's own daughter; Dasharatha invites him to Ayodhya. |
| XI-XIII | The horse sacrifice on the north bank of the Sarayu. |
| XIV | The gods ask Vishnu to be born as a man to end Ravana. |
| XV | A being rises from the fire with a golden vase of nectar; Dasharatha gives it to his three queens. |
| XVI | The gods beget the vanaras and bears. |
| XVII-XVIII | Rishyasringa and Santa return; Dasharatha apologises to Vibhandaka. |
| XIX | The four princes are born. |
| XX-XXI | Vishvamitra comes to Dasharatha and asks for Rama. |

### People
- **Rama** (I). Tall, broad-shouldered, strong of limb; arms reaching below the knee; three
  conch-like lines on the neck; skin "soft and lustrous dark". Keeps his word; obedient, pure,
  eloquent. At birth: "the lotus-eyed, the beauteous boy" (XIX).
  - Born on the **ninth day of Chaitra**, eleven months after the sacrifice (XIX); his name
    means the Delight.
  - **Half** of Vishnu's essence; Bharata a quarter; Lakshmana and Shatrughna share the rest (XIX).
  - Skilled with the bow and in guiding an elephant (XIX).
  - **Game:** the "dusky blue-dark" complexion fits "lustrous dark"; the long arms, the conch
    lines and the lotus eyes should be visible on the realistic Rama.
- **Lakshmana** (XIX). The Auspicious. Cannot bear to eat without Rama; "shares in Vishnu's
  essence".
- **Bharata** (XIX). The Supporter; a quarter of Vishnu; eleven months younger than Rama.
- **Shatrughna** (XIX). Slayer of Foes.
- **Dasharatha** (V, VI). "Lofty-souled", learned in scripture, "like Manu first of kings";
  childless; a king of great wealth, fair-minded and obeyed.
  - **Game:** a mature king, not elderly-frail until Ayodhya Kanda; his anguish at Vishvamitra's
    request (XXI) is real: he faints.
- **Kaushalya, Sumitra, Kaikeyi** (XIII, XV, XIX). Kaushalya slays the sacrificial horse with
  three swords in the rite (XIII). Kaikeyi's father is Ashvapati of Kekaya (XII note).
- **Vasishtha** (VII, XI, XX). The family priest; "faithful to advise"; greets Vishvamitra with a
  smile although they have an old enmity (XX note).
- **Vishvamitra** (XX, XXI). "A mighty saint of glorious fame", son of Gadhi, of Kusika's line.
  - Asks for Rama, "thine eldest born, whom locks like raven's wings adorn" (XXI): **Rama wears
    his hair long and dark.**
  - **No age for Rama** appears in XX or XXI. (The "not yet sixteen" line is expected in XXII;
    see Part B.)
  - Says the rite lasts **ten nights** and the disturbers are **two shape-changing fiends**
    (XXI), later named Maricha and Subahu.
- **Rishyasringa** (VIII-X, XIII-XVIII). Son of the sage Vibhandaka, raised in the forest among
  the deer; knows no woman until lured to Anga. Weds Santa, Dasharatha's daughter. Leads the
  sacrifice and promises Dasharatha four sons.
- **Kusha and Lava** (IV). Twin sons of Rama and Sita, "like Rama's self in face and frame";
  taught by Valmiki.

### Places
- **Ayodhya** (V, VI).
  - On the bank of the Sarayu ("Sarju") in the land of Kosala.
  - **Twelve leagues** long and **three** wide (walls); gates at even intervals; "right glorious
    is her royal street"; belted round with sal trees; a lake for thirsty travellers; seen as "a
    painted city fair with chess-board line and even square"; the name means "not to be
    fought against".
  - Peopled by truthful, pure Brahmins; no poor, thieves or faithless; horses from Bahlika,
    Sindhu, Vanayu and Kamboja; four classes of elephants.
  - **Game:** the courtyard grounds are an invented slice. The wider city should read as
    a grid of broad royal roads and sal-lined avenues beside a river; add sal trees.
- **Anga** (VIII). At the meeting of the Sarayu and the Ganga, near Dasharatha's realm;
  Lomapada's capital is **Champa**, crowned with champaka trees (XVII).
- **The sacrificial ground** (XI-XIII). On the northern bank of the Sarayu.
  - 21 sacrificial posts, "one-and-twenty cubits tall", made of bilva, khadira, palasha, fig and
    devadaru; an altar built "like an eagle with wings of gold".
  - Three days of rites; sixteen priests; huge gifts of cattle, gold and silver.
- **Mithila** (III, XII). The capital of Videha, king Janaka is invited to the sacrifice.
- **The Tamasa** (II). Not far from the Ganga; Valmiki's hermitage is by it.

### Weapons, objects, numbers
- The **sataghni** ("slayer of a hundred") and the archers' bows on Ayodhya's walls (V).
- The **golden vase of nectar** (XV): rises with a dark being "robed in red", "drum-voiced",
  "a tiger in his gait".
- Rama's reign will last **eleven thousand years** (I, XIV).
- Brahma's boon: Ravana can be killed only by a **man** (XIV).

### Notes for the game
- The princes' **ages** at Vishvamitra's arrival are not in XX-XXI.
- **Santa** is Dasharatha's daughter; Rishyasringa's story is a later chapter we do not need.
- **Variants:** the nectar's distribution, the "half of Vishnu" division and the horse sacrifice
  details differ in other tellings; the popular television version simplifies all three.

## Part C. The Ganga, the churning, Vishala and Ahalya (Griffith XXXVI, XXXIX to L)

**Verified** against Griffith. *Griffith's edition leaves out cantos XXXVII and XXXVIII (the glory
of Uma and the birth of Kartikeya, omitted by the translator as unsuited to his readers), so
his numbers after XXXVI are not the popular numbers; popular numbers are marked `pop ?` until
checked.*

### Sequence
| Griffith | What happens |
|---|---|
| XXXVI | The princes and Vishvamitra cross the Sona; at midday they reach the **Ganga**, bathe, and Vishvamitra tells how she was born: elder daughter of Himalaya and Mena, sister of Uma. |
| XXXIX-XLII | The Ikshvaku king **Sagara** of Ayodhya; his two queens (Kesini and Sumati); 60,000 sons from a gourd; Indra steals the sacrificial horse; the sons dig down to the underworld and are burned to ash by Kapila. Sagara's grandson Anshuman recovers the horse; Garuda says only Ganga's water can cleanse them. |
| XLIII-XLIV | Anshuman, Dilipa, then **Bhagiratha**: years of penance; Brahma says Shiva must break the fall; Ganga falls on Shiva's head and is held in his hair, then released in seven streams; Jahnu drinks and releases her; she reaches the ashes. |
| XLV | The princes cross the Ganga by boat and come to **Vishala**. Vishvamitra tells the **churning of the ocean** (Mandara as the pole, Vasuki as the rope, Vishnu as the tortoise; the poison that Shiva drinks; Dhanvantari; the nymphs; Lakshmi; the nectar and the Asuras' defeat). |
| XLVI-XLVII | Diti's wish for a son to kill Indra; Indra cuts the embryo into seven Maruts. Vishvamitra recounts the line of Vishala's kings down to **Sumati**, who comes out to welcome them. |
| XLVIII | Vishvamitra tells of **Indra and Ahalya** in **Gautama's grove** near Mithila. |
| XLIX | **Rama and Lakshmana enter the grove and free Ahalya.** Gautama returns. |
| L | They reach **Janaka's sacrificial ground** at Mithila; twelve days remain before the gods come to the rite. |

### People
- **Ganga** (XXXVI, XLIV). Daughter of Himalaya; "three-pathed", also called Jahnavi, Bhagirathi.
- **Sagara** (XXXIX-XLII). King of Ayodhya, pious and for long childless; his second queen
  *Sumati* (daughter of Arishtanemi) bears a gourd of **60,000 sons**; the first, Kesini,
  bears Asamanja. (A different Sumati from Vishala's king.)
- **Bhagiratha** (XLIII-XLIV). "Above all men for virtue famed"; childless; brings down the Ganga
  after thousands of years of penance, one fast a month, five fires about him.
- **Sumati, king of Vishala** (XLVII). Of the line from Ikshvaku through Alambusha to Vishala,
  the founder of the city. He comes out "girt with his priest and lords", "with suppliant
  hands, with head inclined", greets Vishvamitra: "High fate is mine, great anchorite."
  - **No age or looks are given.** The game's Sumati is invented in appearance; mark as such.
- **Gautama** (XLVIII-XLIX). "Holy hermit"; "in splendour like the burning flame"; his wrath
  "the gods and fiends would shun". His eldest son is **Shatananda**, chaplain of Janaka (L).
- **Ahalya** (XLVIII-XLIX). Called "the fair Ahalya". **She is not turned to stone.**
  - Indra comes disguised as Gautama; she recognises him and consents.
  - Gautama's curse on her: "Thy bed the ashes, air thy food, here shalt thou live in
    solitude", and **"not an eye thy form shall see"**: she lives unseen, in penance.
  - She will be freed when Rama comes: "his coming shall remove thy stain".
  - Rama and Lakshmana enter the grove; she is "screened from eye of man and God and fiend";
    they touch her feet; she receives them with water; the gods rejoice; Gautama returns and
    takes her back, "from sin and folly purified".
  - **Game:** matches mission 6 (invisible, ash and air, freed by Rama's coming, feet touched,
    Gautama returns). Keep the stone version in the codex only.
- **Indra** (XLVIII). Cursed "sexless"; his vigour leaves him.
- **Vasuki, Mandara, Vishnu as tortoise, Shiva** (XLV): the poison drunk by Shiva; **Dhanvantari**
  rises with the pot of amrita; Lakshmi "leapt to land".

### Places
- **The Ganga at the confluence stretch** (XXXVI): Sarasa cranes and swans fill the river.
  Himalaya, "the mighty hill with metals stored".
- **Vishala** (XLV, XLVII): across the Ganga from the princes' crossing; founded by Vishala son
  of Alambusha and Ikshvaku; the place where "Lord Mahendra dwelt".
- **Gautama's grove** (XLVIII-XLIX): near Mithila, "most lovely then"; after the curse, a place
  hidden from sight. Gautama goes to the Himalaya heights.
- **Janaka's sacrificial ground** (L).

### Weapons, objects, numbers
- 60,000 sons, a league's digging each (XL); thirty thousand years (XLII).
- **A thousand years** of churning (XLV); sixty million nymphs rise.
- Vishnu's shell, mace, discus and the **Sarnga** bow (XLV).
- Seven Maruts (XLVII).
- Twelve days to Janaka's rite (L).

### What differs from the popular picture
- **Ahalya is unseen, not stone**; her trial is austerity (see above).
- The **churning** is told here at Vishala, not in a separate place.
- Griffith omits the **Uma** and **Kartikeya** cantos, which the popular text includes.
- **Variants:** the stone Ahalya (Ramcharitmanas, later Puranas, Kamba differences); the
  critical edition drops part of the Vishala material.

## Part D. Vishvamitra's story (Griffith LI to LXIV)

**Verified.** Told at Mithila by Shatananda and Vishvamitra before the princes.

### Sequence
| Griffith | What happens |
|---|---|
| LI | Shatananda tells Vishvamitra's lineage (Kusha, Gadhi) and asks about his mother Ahalya. Vishvamitra, a king, visits **Vasishtha's hermitage** with an army. |
| LII-LIII | Vasishtha feeds the whole army through his wish-cow ("Dapple-skin"); Vishvamitra tries to buy her with elephants, chariots, horses and "a hundred thousand kine"; Vasishtha refuses. |
| LIV-LV | Vishvamitra takes her by force; she produces warriors that destroy his army and his hundred sons; he gives his kingdom away and goes to the Himalaya to win weapons from Shiva; he burns Vasishtha's grove. |
| LVI | Vasishtha's wand swallows every divine weapon; Vishvamitra: "Warriors' strength is poor and slight; a Brahman's power is truly might." He resolves to win Brahmanhood by penance. |
| LVII-LX | **Trishanku** wants to rise to heaven in his body; Vasishtha's sons curse him; Vishvamitra holds a rite for him; Indra throws him down; Vishvamitra makes new southern stars. |
| LXI-LXII | **Shunahshepha** saved from a sacrifice by two hymns taught by Vishvamitra. |
| LXIII-LXIV | **Menaka** (ten years' distraction) and **Rambha** (cursed to stone for ten thousand years). Vishvamitra vows to hold his breath for a thousand years. |

### People
- **Vishvamitra** (LI-LXIV). Gadhi's son, born a king (of Kusha's line); loses his sons and
  kingdom; wins Brahmin rank only after **thousands of years** of austerity (breath held,
  one foot, arms raised, air as food, five fires in summer); cursed his own sons and the sons
  of Vasishtha; fell to Menaka and Rambha's trials and recovered.
  - **Game:** a gaunt, fierce ascetic whose body is "dry as a log of wood" (LXV); long
    hair; no softness. The warm, patient teacher in the road scenes is a **development**:
    show the fierceness in his stillness and look.
- **Vasishtha** (LII-LVI). "Son of Brahma"; his Brahma-wand defeats every weapon; his wish-cow
  "Dapple-skin" ("all spot without, all pure within").
- **Trishanku** (LVII-LX). Of Ikshvaku's line; cursed to look like an outcast ("swart of hue",
  with an iron ring); raised among the stars head downward.
- **Menaka** (LXIII) "the fairest of the fair"; **Rambha** (LXIV) cursed to stone.

### Places
- Vasishtha's hermitage (LI): trees, flowers, creepers; deer forget their fear; "like
  Brahma's own seat".
- The Himalaya (LV); a land far to the south (LVII); **Pushkara** and its lake (LXI-LXIII);
  **Kaushiki** and the northern snow hills (LXIII).

### Numbers
- Vishvamitra's army offer: twice seven thousand tusked elephants, eight hundred four-horse
  cars, eleven thousand steeds, ten million heifers (LIII).
- A hundred sons (LV); four sons in the south (LVII); a thousand years (LVII, LXIII), ten
  years with Menaka (LXIII); ten thousand years in stone for Rambha (LXIV).
- Vasishtha's hundred sons (LVIII), seven hundred rebirths (LIX).

### What differs from the popular picture
- The wish-cow is not named **Shabala** in Griffith.
- **Vasishtha's sons** (not Vasishtha) curse Trishanku.
- Several of Vishvamitra's tales are **darker** than in children's retellings.
- **Game use:** codex leaves only (*Kaushika and the cow*, *Trishanku's stars*, *Menaka*), told
  with restraint.

## Part E. Mithila: the bow, the weddings and Parashurama (Griffith LXV to LXXVII)

All **verified** against Griffith (cantos read in full). No age is stated for anyone in these
cantos.

### Sequence
| Griffith | What happens |
|---|---|
| LXV | Vishvamitra's thousand-year silent penance in the east; Indra, disguised as a Brahmin, begs his meal and he fasts again; Brahma and Vasishtha confirm him a Brahmin sage. Janaka praises him. |
| LXVI | At dawn Janaka receives Vishvamitra and the princes. The sage asks to see the bow. Janaka tells its history, how he found Sita in the furrow, the failed suitors and the year-long siege of Mithila; Sita will go to whoever strings it. |
| LXVII | **Five thousand youths** haul out the bow in an **eight-wheeled iron chest**. Rama opens it, lifts the bow by its middle and bends it until it **breaks in two**. The thunder makes the crowd faint; only the king, the sage and the two princes stay conscious. Janaka pledges Sita and sends envoys. |
| LXVIII | The envoys ride **three nights** to Ayodhya; Dasharatha agrees to go to Mithila the next day. |
| LXIX | The king goes with ministers, priests and a four-fold host; **four days** to Videha's border. Janaka meets him. |
| LXX-LXXI | Janaka's younger brother **Kushadhwaja** of Sankasya (on the Ikshumati) is summoned. Vasishtha recites the Ikshvaku line; Janaka recites his own, including a war with Sudhanva of Sankasya, who had demanded the bow and Sita. |
| LXXII | Marriages arranged: Sita to Rama, **Urmila to Lakshmana**, and Kushadhwaja's daughters **Mandavi to Bharata** and **Shrutakirti to Shatrughna**; a gift of cattle; weddings on the last day of Phalguna. |
| LXXIII | Yudhajit, Bharata's uncle, arrives. The four weddings are performed on one day at the sacrificial ground: Janaka pours holy water over Sita's hand into Rama's; the couples circle the fire three times. |
| LXXIV | On the way home a storm rises and **Parashurama** appears, axe on shoulder, bow ready. |
| LXXV | He challenges Rama with Vishnu's bow; Dasharatha pleads in vain. |
| LXXVI | Rama takes the bow, strings it, sets an arrow, and will not kill a Brahmin; the arrow closes the heavenly worlds Parashurama had won. He departs. |
| LXXVII | Rama returns the bow to Varuna. Ayodhya welcomes the brides. Bharata and Shatrughna go to their uncle Yudhajit. |

### People
- **Janaka** (LXVI-LXXIII). King of Videha, "lord of virtuous mind"; a ploughman-king who found
  an infant in a furrow ("Sita" means "furrow"). He does not choose a husband: he **vowed her to
  whoever strings the bow** (LXVI). He fought and killed Sudhanva of Sankasya "with a single hand"
  (LXXI).
- **Sita** (LXVI, LXXIII, LXXVII).
  - Not born of woman; found in the furrow: "my cherished daughter, fair to view".
  - Descriptions are few and brief: "beautiful to view", "bright in rich attire" at the wedding
    (LXXIII); "more goddess-fair in form and face", "the loveliest wife that e'er was seen",
    "lofty-fated dame" (LXXVII), ending the book by comparing Rama and Sita to Vishnu and
    Lakshmi.
  - **Game:** no complexion, height or hair is given in these cantos. Choose with restraint and
    mark as invented; compare her Aranya Kanda description before finalising.
- **Urmila, Mandavi, Shrutakirti** (LXXI-LXXIII). Janaka's daughter Urmila weds Lakshmana;
  Kushadhwaja's daughters weds Bharata and Shatrughna.
- **Vishvamitra** (LXV). A saint "pious-souled"; his fasting body "dry as a log of wood"; became a
  Brahmin sage after a thousand years of silent penance in the east.
- **Parashurama** (LXXIV, LXXV). "Jamadagni's son", "Bhrigu's son", with "long hair twisted round
  his head", "firm as Kailasa's hill", "fierce as the fire of doom", axe on shoulder. Wins and loses:
  he **gave the earth to Kashyapa and lives on Mount Mahendra**.
- **Rama** (LXVII, LXXVI). Acts "as if in sport"; "lotus-eyed"; unhurried before the court.
- **Dasharatha** (LXXV). Before Parashurama "with pallid cheek and timid eyes".

### Places
- **Mithila** (LXVI-LXXIII). Capital of Videha; the sacrifice and the weddings are held on "the
  fair ground". The text says little about the city; the bow is kept in a chest hauled by hand.
- **Sankasya** (LXX). Kushadhwaja's city, "decked with grace divine", on the **Ikshumati**.
- **Mahendra** (LXXV). The mountain where Parashurama retires.
- **The road home** (LXXIV): birds and beasts flee, a dust storm, trees uprooted.
- **Ayodhya** (LXXVII): "gay pennons", drums and flowers; the palace "like Himalaya's mountain".

### Weapons, objects, numbers
- **The bow of Shiva** (LXVI-LXVII, LXXV): "this heavenly bow, exceeding bright"; Rudra's bow
  from Daksha's sacrifice; handed down to Devarata. Heavy: five thousand men and eight wheels.
  **Rama breaks it.**
- **Vishnu's bow** (LXXV): the second of the two bows made by the divine craftsman; given to
  Richika, then Jamadagni, then Parashurama. Rama strings it (LXXVI) and returns it to Varuna
  (LXXVII).
- **Dowry** (LXXIV): silks, a warrior force, chariots, elephants, horses, maids and slaves,
  silver, coral, gold and pearls; cattle given to the Brahmins (LXXII).
- **Numbers:** 5,000 youths (LXVII); three nights (LXVIII), four days (LXIX); a year-long siege
  (LXVI); a thousand years (LXV).

### What differs from the popular picture
- **It is not a swayamvara** where Sita chooses: it is a test of strength set by her father,
  *and the whole court is present* when Rama breaks the bow (LXVII); the crowd faints.
  The game's STORY.md currently says Janaka shows the bow to Rama alone: **to correct** in the
  audit.
- **The bow breaks** rather than merely being strung.
- **Sita is not described** beyond a few words.
- **All four brothers marry on the same day**, and Mandavi and Shrutakirti are cousins of Sita.
- **Parashurama's confrontation** happens on the return journey, with Vishnu's bow, not at the
  wedding.
- **Variants:** Tulsidas' Sita garlands Rama in a swayamvara; the critical edition differs
  slightly in the order of the bow's history.
