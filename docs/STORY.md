# Story Bible — *Ramayana: The Journey of Rama*

> Companion to the [GDD](GDD.md). Primary source: Valmiki Ramayana. All dialogue here is original
> working text for the cultural consultant to review, not quoted from any translation.
> Regional variants (Kamba Ramayanam, Ramcharitmanas, Ranganatha, Krittivasi) go into the
> **lore codex**, not the main storyline.

---

> **Release structure:** the game ships as 8 episodic Parts (see GDD §10).
> - Parts 1–5 follow Bala, Ayodhya, Aranya, Kishkindha and Sundara Kanda.
> - Yuddha Kanda is split into Part 6 (*The Bridge to Lanka*: Setu, Kumbhakarna) and Part 7
>   (*The War for Dharma*: Indrajit, Ravana).
> - Part 8 (*The Return*) is the Uttara Kanda finale.

## 1. Narrative Pillars, Tone and Framing

### Pillars
- **Dharma is shown through choices, not speeches.** Rama's restraint, obedience and compassion
  appear in play: sparing a fleeing foe, shielding a sage, putting the bow down. The Dharma meter
  shows it; the story never lectures.
- **Rama grows from prince to protector.** Bala Kanda is a coming of age: a gifted boy is given
  power (the astras) only after he shows the judgement to use it.
- **Reverence with warmth.** Divine figures are calm and dignified. Warmth comes from the
  brothers' bond, the sages' affection and Ayodhya's people. No comedy around Rama, Sita or
  Hanuman.
- **Mythic, not gory.** Rakshasas dissolve into smoke and ember when defeated. Astras are shown
  as cosmic forces, never as bloody spectacle.
- **Every leaf is a story.** Palm-leaf manuscripts reward curiosity with history, regional
  retellings and the meaning of names and places.

### Framing device

| Option | How it works | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Lava and Kusha sing the Ramayana** (taught by Valmiki; they recite it before Rama in the Uttara Kanda) | Kanda openings and loading screens show the twins at Valmiki's ashram, then at Rama's court, singing to a veena | Faithful to Valmiki's own frame. The game ends where the frame began, which gives the Uttara Kanda a quiet, earned ending | The twins' identity is a late reveal, so their early scenes must stay understated |
| B. Narada tells Valmiki (the opening of Bala Kanda) | Narada describes the ideal man; Valmiki composes | Faithful, short | Little emotional payoff; Narada is absent afterwards |
| C. No narrator; Rama's point of view | Pure in-world cutscenes | Simple to produce | Loses the oral-tradition texture and the help a frame gives for difficult episodes |

**Recommendation: A.** Two young voices singing at Valmiki's ashram open each kanda (30–45 s,
skippable). Valmiki himself narrates codex entries. In the final kanda the camera finally shows
whom the twins are singing to. A frame also gives us a respectful way to compress or summarise
sensitive episodes ("and so it is sung…") without playing them.

## 2. Bala Kanda — Vertical Slice Beat Sheet

### The Agneyastra problem
The prototype gives young Rama **Agneyastra** at Ayodhya. In Valmiki, Vishvamitra gives Rama the
divine astras *after* Tataka is slain (the GDD currently grants them in Mission 2, before
Tataka). Proposal:

1. **The prologue is one of Vasishtha's maya trials.** The rakshasas are illusions in dark smoke,
   as the prototype already shows. In the final wave Vasishtha lets Rama *glimpse* Agneyastra:
   "This is not yet yours. Another teacher will place it in your hands. Today, learn how heavy it
   is." When the trial ends the astra slot goes dark again.
2. **Move the real astra unlocks to after Tataka.** Mission 2 instead grants the **Bala and
   Atibala** mantras, which Valmiki has Vishvamitra teach on the bank of the Sarayu (they free one
   from hunger and fatigue). In play this is a stamina and dodge-recovery buff. Tataka is then
   fought with bow and dodge only, which fits the GDD's goal of "teaching dodge timing".

This keeps the prototype's content, teaches astras early, and stays faithful.

### Mission beats

**1. Golden Hour (prologue — current prototype).**
*Beat:* Golden light on the training grounds of Ayodhya. Rama and Lakshmana practise at the
dummies while Guru Vasishtha watches from the pavilion. He summons maya rakshasas from dark smoke
to test them: three waves. In the last one he lets Rama glimpse Agneyastra.
*Cutscenes:* Opening flyover of Ayodhya and the Sarayu; Lakshmana handing Rama an arrow; after the
trial, a herald runs in: "A sage stands at the palace gate — Vishvamitra of Kaushika's line."
*Lines:*
- Vasishtha: "An arrow obeys the hand. The hand must obey something higher."
- Lakshmana: "Brother, leave one for me. Just one."
- Rama (after the Agni glimpse): "It burned without touching me, Guru-ji. Is that what power is?"
- Vasishtha: "That is what *restraint* is. Remember the feeling."

**2. The Sage at the Gate.**
*Beat:* Vishvamitra asks Dasharatha for Rama to guard his yagna against Maricha and Subahu.
The king, grieving at the thought, offers his army instead; Vishvamitra's anger rises; Vasishtha
counsels the king to trust the sage. Rama and Lakshmana leave with Vishvamitra. On the bank of
the Sarayu he teaches them Bala and Atibala. They pass the ashram of Kama and cross the Ganga.
*Gameplay:* Short court cutscene, then a walking traversal with palm-leaf pickups and a
Lakshmana Guard-command tutorial against forest animals scared by distant rakshasas (no kills).
*Lines:*
- Dasharatha: "He is not yet sixteen. Take my army, take me — not my son."
- Vishvamitra: "A king who promises and then bargains has forgotten whose son he is."
- Vasishtha: "Let him go, my king. With this sage, no harm can reach him."
- Rama: "Father, I will return. Bless the bow, not only the boy."

**3. The Silent Forest.**
*Beat:* The once-prosperous lands of Malada and Karusha lie empty. Vishvamitra tells the story of
Tataka, the yakshini who became a terror after a sage's curse, and of her son Maricha.
*Gameplay:* Exploration, the first *real* rakshasas (unlike the maya ones, they leave embers,
not smoke). The **Dharma meter** is introduced: a wounded rakshasa that flees can be spared.
*Lines:*
- Vishvamitra: "No bird sings here. Listen to what silence costs a land."
- Rama (sparing a fleeing foe): "Go. Do not return."

**4. Boss: Tataka.**
*Beat:* Rama hesitates to strike a woman. Vishvamitra answers that protecting the innocent is the
duty that matters here, citing earlier guardians of dharma who had to act the same way. Rama
first tries to drive her off without killing her; only when she keeps attacking with illusion
and stones does he end the fight.
*Gameplay:* Charge, ground slam, boulder throw; enrages at 50% and turns invisible (maya) — her
position is shown by stones falling and dust. Bow and dodge only.
*Cutscene:* After the fight the forest blooms again. At dawn, Vishvamitra, pleased, gives Rama
the divine astras in a ceremony: each astra appears as a radiant figure and bows to Rama.
*Lines:*
- Rama: "Guru-dev, she is a woman. Is there no other way?"
- Vishvamitra: "Ask instead: how many have no other way while she lives? A protector's duty does
  not look away."
- The astras (as one voice): "Command us, and we will come."

**5. Six Nights at Siddhashrama.**
*Beat:* At Vishvamitra's hermitage the sages begin a six-night yagna in silence. On the last
night Maricha and Subahu attack from the sky.
*Gameplay:* Astra tutorials in the ashram (Varunastra puts out fires, Vayavyastra clears
crowds), then wave defence around the altar. **Dharma bonus** for keeping the sacred fire and
the sages unhurt.
*Boss:* Subahu falls to Agneyastra. Maricha is **not killed**: Rama casts him far over the ocean,
a deliberate act of mercy that Valmiki records (and that sets up the golden deer in Aranya
Kanda). Valmiki names the astra used on Maricha as the **Manavastra**; the GDD uses Vayavyastra
(see §6).
*Lines:*
- Rama: "This one I will not kill. Let the sea teach him what mercy is."
- Lakshmana: "He will remember this, brother."
- Rama: "Then let him remember that he was spared."

**6. The Stone and the Dawn.**
*Beat:* On the road to Mithila, Vishvamitra tells the story of Ganga's descent (codex). At
Gautama's empty ashram Rama's arrival ends Ahalya's long penance; in Valmiki she has been
living unseen, not turned to stone (the stone version is a later tradition, so codex note). The
brothers touch her feet; Gautama returns and is reunited with her.
*Gameplay:* No combat. A gentle exploration level: overgrown ashram, light that brightens as Rama
approaches. Punya reward for finding all palm leaves.
*Lines:*
- Ahalya: "So many seasons. I had forgotten the sound of footsteps."
- Rama: "Mother, your penance is complete. It is we who are blessed to see it."

**7. The Bow of Shiva.**
*Beat:* King Janaka welcomes them and tells how Shiva's bow came to his family, and that whoever
can string it wins Sita's hand. Five thousand men wheel in its iron chest. Rama lifts it, strings
it, and as he draws it the bow breaks with a sound like thunder. Janaka gives Sita's hand;
messengers ride to Ayodhya; the four brothers marry the four princesses.
*Gameplay:* A cinematic **rhythm/QTE** built on the draw-hold-release mechanic: lift (hold),
string (trace), draw (the longest perfect window in the game). The break is a cutscene; failure
just replays the moment, Rama never "fails" visibly.
*Note:* Valmiki presents this as a *viryashulka* (a test of strength), not a swayamvara with
rival suitors; the popular swayamvara with assembled kings (e.g. Ramcharitmanas) goes into the
codex.
*Lines:*
- Janaka: "Many kings came to lift it. They left lighter than they arrived."
- Rama: "With your leave, Guru-dev, let me only touch it."
- Sita (first sight, a single line at the wedding; Valmiki gives her no speech in Bala Kanda, so
  this is a game addition for the consultant to approve): "The bow shook the sky, and yet your hand
  is steady."

## 3. Character Arcs and Voice Notes

| Character | Arc in Bala Kanda (and beyond) | Voice notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Rama** | Gifted prince → protector who earns power by restraint; questions, then accepts hard duty (Tataka); chooses mercy (Maricha) | Calm, few words, warm with family, formal with elders. Never boasts, never quips. Asks real questions. |
| **Lakshmana** | Eager younger brother → Rama's shield. Impulsive but utterly loyal | Quicker, more emotional, protective; light warmth allowed, never mocking Rama |
| **Vishvamitra** | Demanding mentor who tests, then gives everything (the astras) | Fierce, terse, proud; a former king who became a brahmarshi — his history is codex material |
| **Vasishtha** | Rama's first guru; values character over power | Gentle, measured, aphoristic. Frames the prologue's maya trials |
| **Dasharatha** | Loving father who can't let go (foreshadows Ayodhya Kanda) | Tender, anxious, regal; voice cracks with feeling |
| **Sita** | In Bala: brief, dignified introduction — Janaka's daughter, found in the furrow of the earth. Later: the heart of the journey, with her own will and strength | Composed, perceptive, kind. Never a prize: she sees Rama as clearly as he sees her |
| **Tataka** | A yakshini cursed into ferocity; her defeat restores the land | Low growls and few words; treated as a tragic, cursed being, not mocked |
| **Maricha** | Spared in Bala; returns as the golden deer in Aranya. Mercy with consequences | Arrogant at first; his fear of Rama later in the epic is foreshadowed here |
| **Subahu** | The cruel one, who destroys the yagna with fire and blood | Mocking and boastful (rakshasas may be arrogant; Rama never answers in kind) |
| **Ahalya** | Long penance ends; dignity restored | Quiet, grateful, luminous. No focus on her past transgression |
| **Janaka** | A philosopher-king; Sita's father; deep respect for Rama | Warm, wise, slightly ceremonial |

## 4. Kandas 2–7

**2. Ayodhya Kanda — *The Weight of a Promise*.** Rama's coronation is announced; Kaikeyi, swayed
by Manthara, claims her two boons: Bharata as king and fourteen years of exile for Rama. Rama
accepts without anger; Sita and Lakshmana insist on going with him. *Throughline:* duty above
desire, and the grief it leaves behind. *Set pieces:* walking through a weeping Ayodhya; crossing
the Ganga with Guha at night; building the hut at Chitrakoot. *Climax (no boss):* Bharata arrives
to beg Rama to return and leaves carrying Rama's padukas to place on the throne — a dialogue
scene whose moral choices fill the Dharma meter.

**3. Aranya Kanda — *The Forest Takes Its Price*.** Sages of Dandaka ask for protection; Rama
fights Viradha and receives divine weapons from Agastya (**Brahmastra** unlock). At Panchavati,
Shurpanakha's rejection brings Khara and Dushana's 14,000 (wave survival). Ravana sends Maricha as
the golden deer; Sita is taken. Jatayu dies trying to save her. *Throughline:* a peaceful life
broken; Rama's first true grief. *Set pieces:* the deer chase; the Khara wave defence; Jatayu's
final flight (playable summon, then his death as a cutscene); Shabari's hospitality.
*Bosses:* Khara; Maricha (deer).

**4. Kishkindha Kanda — *Allies and Hard Choices*.** Hanuman meets the brothers; Rama allies with
Sugriva, wronged by his brother Vali. *Throughline:* friendship and the cost of a promise.
*Set pieces:* climbing Rishyamukha; the seven sala trees pierced by one arrow (a perfect-release
showcase); the search parties set out. *Boss:* **Vali** (a duel framed with care, see §5). Unlocks
Hanuman and Vanara summons.

**5. Sundara Kanda — *Hope Across the Sea*.** Told as Hanuman "visions" seen from Rama's side.
*Throughline:* hope and devotion. *Set pieces:* the ocean leap; finding Sita at Ashoka Vatika
(Hanuman offers Rama's ring); Lanka burning. *Boss (vision):* Akshaya Kumara; Indrajit binds
Hanuman with the Brahmastra in a cutscene. Ends with Rama holding Sita's chudamani.

**6. Yuddha Kanda — *The Bridge and the War*.** Vibhishana seeks refuge and Rama welcomes him; the
Vanaras build the Setu. *Throughline:* righteous war fought without hatred. *Set pieces:* Setu
building/defence; Lakshmana struck down and Hanuman bringing the mountain of herbs; Agastya
teaching Aditya Hridayam before the final day. *Bosses:* Kumbhakarna, Indrajit (illusion fight),
**Ravana** (multi-phase, regrowing heads; Brahmastra finale). **Narayanastra** is the ultimate,
though Valmiki does not show Rama using it in this war, so flag for the consultant.

**7. Uttara Kanda — *The Song Returns*.** Coronation and Rama Rajya. The framing device closes:
Lava and Kusha sing before Rama, and he recognises his sons. *Throughline:* the cost of kingship
and the endurance of the story. Mostly cinematic and exploratory (a prosperous Ayodhya), with boss
rush and New Game+ unlocked. Handle the later events as in §5.

## 5. Sensitive Moments

| Moment | Risk | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Tataka is a woman** | Seen as glorifying violence against women | Keep Rama's hesitation and Vishvamitra's reasoning on screen. Show her as a cursed yakshini, not a sexualised or mocked figure. Rama first tries to drive her off. Her death dissolves into light; the land blooms. |
| **Ahalya** | Victim-blaming or sexualised retelling | Focus on her penance and Rama's reverence. Leave Indra's deception to a short, neutral codex leaf. |
| **Shurpanakha** | Mutilation; mocking a woman's desire | No gameplay. Cut away before Lakshmana strikes (the wound is implied by her cry and her return to Khara). No comedy in her approach to Rama. Codex notes the varying regional readings. |
| **Vali's killing from cover** | Rama appears unfair | Not a "gotcha". Play Sugriva's two duels; the player shoots only on a story prompt, and Valmiki's full exchange (Vali's accusation, Rama's answer, Vali's acceptance and his entrusting of Angada) plays uncut. Let the player feel the weight; don't resolve it for them. |
| **Sita's abduction** | Assault imagery | Ravana's disguise and the chariot rising; no struggle close-ups. Jatayu's fight is the action. |
| **Agni pariksha** | Seen as Rama doubting or punishing Sita | Never a mechanic or QTE. Sita's choice and voice lead the scene; Agni himself returns her and declares her purity, and Rama states that he never doubted her but the world must witness it. Consultant sign-off required. |
| **Uttara Kanda: Sita's banishment, Shambuka** | Deep, ongoing debate; parts often seen as later additions | Do not play them. Lava-Kusha's song tells them briefly; Sita's return to the earth is shown as her own choice, with dignity. Shambuka: codex only, or omit (see §6). |
| **Depicting Rama and Hanuman** | Devotional sensitivity | No humour, no costume skins, no defeat animations that humiliate; Rama "rests" rather than dies on a fail state. |
| **Rakshasas as a people** | Dehumanising a group | Show individual characters (Vibhishana, Mandodari, Trijata) with dignity; Ravana as a learned scholar undone by pride. |

## 6. Open Decisions for the Game Owner

1. **Framing device.**
   (a) Lava and Kusha singing, (b) Narada telling Valmiki, (c) no narrator.
   *Recommend (a):* faithful, and it gives a respectful ending to the Uttara Kanda.
2. **Agneyastra in the prologue.**
   (a) Prologue is a maya trial; Agneyastra is a temporary "glimpse" removed afterwards and
   granted for real after Tataka. (b) Keep the prologue as is and present it as a flash-forward
   vision of the yagna. (c) Remove Agneyastra from the prologue.
   *Recommend (a):* keeps the prototype content and teaches astras early without breaking canon.
   It also means moving the GDD's astra unlocks from Mission 2 to after Tataka.
3. **Which astra sends Maricha over the sea?**
   (a) Manavastra, as in Valmiki (a new, non-lethal "banishing" astra used only in this fight).
   (b) Vayavyastra, as in the GDD, with a codex note.
   *Recommend (a)* as a scripted, one-time astra; Vayavyastra still clears the other rakshasas,
   which matches Valmiki.
4. **Shiva's bow: swayamvara or test of strength?**
   (a) Valmiki's version: Janaka shows the bow to Rama only. (b) The popular swayamvara with
   rival kings failing first. *Recommend (a)* for the scene, with the rival-kings version in the
   codex; it is shorter and keeps the focus on Rama and Sita.
5. **Parashurama encounter (end of Bala Kanda).**
   (a) A playable mission 8 (a non-lethal duel of wills, a rhythm draw of Vishnu's bow). (b)
   A cutscene epilogue. (c) Skip. *Recommend (b)* for the free slice, as a teaser for the paid
   chapters; a full duel of two avatars of Vishnu risks feeling wrong as combat.
6. **Uttara Kanda scope.**
   (a) End with the coronation and Lava-Kusha reveal; later events only in the song. (b) Adapt
   the full kanda, including the banishment and Sita's return to the earth. (c) End at the
   coronation and leave Uttara as codex only. *Recommend (a):* complete, faithful to the frame,
   and avoids putting the most debated episodes under the player's control.
